Speech · Tinnitus · Cognition

How we hear one voice among many

The Speech, Tinnitus, and Cognition Lab at San Diego State University studies how listeners follow a single talker when the world is full of competing sound — and how hearing, language experience, and cognition shape who can do it well.

Explore our research

What we do

Speech

How listeners follow one voice among many, how that ability differs across languages, and how hearing loss and hearing devices change it.

  • Speech perception in multi-talker environments
  • Cross-language and cross-linguistic listening
  • Hearing loss and hearing devices
  • Speech denoising using deep learning

Tinnitus

What drives the perception of sound without an external source, and what can be done to relieve it.

  • Mechanisms
  • Intervention

Cognition

Listening is an effortful, cognitive act. We study the cognitive side of hearing, and speech-based measures that may aid clinical screening.

  • How cognition predicts speech understanding
  • Cognition in individuals with neurological disorders (TBI)

Principal Investigator

Xianhui (Kaye) Wang, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor · School of Speech, Language, and Hearing Sciences

Dr. Wang directs the STC Lab at San Diego State University. Her research examines speech perception in challenging listening conditions across languages and populations, with a focus on the interplay between auditory and cognitive processing. She received her Ph.D. in Hearing Science from Ohio University and completed postdoctoral training in the Department of Otolaryngology – Head and Neck Surgery at the University of California, Irvine.
